## Énoncé et corrigé fourni


In an Azure subscription named Subscription1, there is a resource group named RG1.
Inside RG1, two load balancers are deployed:
- LB1 – configured as an internal load balancer
- LB2 – configured as a public load balancer
You need to grant Admin1 the ability to create a health probe on LB2. The solution must follow the principle of least privilege.
Which Azure RBAC role should assignment be assigned to Admin1 to allow the creation of a health probe on LB2?
Réponses:
☐ Contributor role assigned at the LB2 scope
**☑** Network Contributor role assigned at the LB2 scope
☐ Network Contributor role assigned at the RG1 scope
☐ Owner role assigned at the LB2 scope
Explication
`Network Contributor role assigned at the LB2 scope` is correct because it adheres to the principle of least privilege. This role provides sufficient permissions to configure and manage health probes on the specific load balancer without granting unnecessary access to the entire resource group or other unrelated network resources. The Network Contributor role at the resource group level ensures Admin1 has the necessary access to both the load balancer and the related network resources, adhering to the principle of least privilege.
`Network Contributor role assigned at the RG1 scope` is incorrect because assigning this role at the resource group level grants broader permissions than necessary. While it enables the required operations, it goes against the principle of least privilege by providing access to all network resources within the resource group, not just LB2.
`Contributor role assigned at the LB2 scope` is incorrect because it grants excessive permissions, including access to non-network-related tasks, violating the principle of least privilege.
`Owner role assigned at the LB2 scope` is incorrect because the Owner role provides full administrative control, including the ability to assign roles, which far exceeds the permissions needed to add a health probe.
