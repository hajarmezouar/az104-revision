## Énoncé et corrigé fourni


You have an Azure subscription that includes the virtual networks listed below:
- VNet1 in the West US region, peered with VNet2
- VNet2 in the West US region, peered with VNet1 and VNet3
- VNet3 in the East US region, peered with VNet2
The subscription also contains the following virtual machines:
- VM1, connected to VNet1
- VM2, connected to VNet2
- VM3, connected to VNet3
All virtual machines have private IP addresses only.
You deploy an Azure Bastion host named Bastion1 in VNet1.
Which virtual machines can you connect to by using Bastion1?
Réponses:
☐ VM1 only
**☑** VM1 and VM2 only
☐ VM1 and VM3 only
☐ VM1, VM2, and VM3
Explication
`VM1 and VM2 only` is the correct answer because Azure Bastion allows connections to virtual machines within the same virtual network or peered virtual networks. Since Bastion1 is deployed in VNet1, it can connect to VM1 (in VNet1) and VM2 (in VNet2), as VNet1 and VNet2 are peered. However, VM3 is in VNet3, which is peered only with VNet2 but not directly with VNet1, preventing Bastion1 from connecting to VM3.
`VM1 only` is incorrect because Bastion1 can also connect to VM2 in VNet2 through the peering with VNet1.
`VM1 and VM3 only` is incorrect because, while VM1 is accessible, VM3 is not reachable via Bastion1 due to the lack of direct peering between VNet1 and VNet3.
`VM1, VM2, and VM3` is incorrect because Bastion1 cannot connect to VM3 since there is no direct peering between VNet1 and VNet3.

- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/bastion/vnet-peering
- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-network/virtual-network-peering-overview
