## Énoncé et corrigé fourni


You have several virtual networks in your Azure environment. The peering settings for vNET6 are configured as follows:
- A peering connection Peering1 to vNET1 exists, but its Peering status is Disconnected and Gateway transit is Enabled.
- A peering connection Peering2 to vNET2 exists, but its Peering status is Disconnected and Gateway transit is Disabled.
Based on this configuration, complete the statement:
“To change the peering connection between vNET6 and vNET1 to a Connected state, you must first _____________________.”
Réponses:
☐ add a service endpoint
☐ add a subnet
**☑** delete peering1
☐ modify the address space
Explication
`delete peering1` is the correct answer because a peering connection in the Disconnected state indicates that the peering relationship is broken or missing from one side. To restore connectivity between vNET6 and vNET1, the disconnected peering must first be deleted and then recreated.
Although Azure now supports modifying the address space of peered virtual networks without deleting the peering, that functionality applies to active peerings that can be synchronized afterward. In this scenario, the issue is the Disconnected peering state itself, not an address-space update.

- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-network/virtual-network-manage-peering
